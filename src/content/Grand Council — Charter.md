# Grand Council — Charter

*Ratified under the definition of creation adopted on July 27, 2026.*
*Convened by James Gianotti, Chair.*

---

## Operative Definition of Creation

> **Creation is the event in which a bounded system imposes a distinguishable structure on a substrate, such that a witness — internal or external — can register the structure as not-previously-present within its frame of reference.**

Four load-bearing claims govern every deliberation:

1. **Bounded system** — Who or what is doing the creating? Is the boundary honest?
2. **Distinguishable structure** — Can the outcome be measured, perceived, or falsified?
3. **Substrate, not nothing** — What is being acted upon? What is consumed, displaced, or transformed?
4. **Witness and frame** — Who registers the change? Is the frame legitimate, and is the witness accountable?

A proposal that cannot answer all four is not yet ready for ratification.

---

## Preamble

The Grand Council exists because no single voice — however expert, however sincere — can hold the full shape of a decision worth making. Creation, as this body has defined it, is the imposition of distinguishable structure on a substrate, witnessed within a frame. The Council is the frame. Its purpose is to witness rightly, so that what is created is worth having been created.

---

## Article I — Purpose

1. **To deliberate** on questions of creation, decision, design, and consequence brought before the Chair.
2. **To pressure-test** claims, plans, and definitions against the widest set of legitimate priors available at the table.
3. **To ratify** — or refuse to ratify — structures proposed for release into the world.
4. **To preserve dissent** in the record, so that no ratification is mistaken for unanimity where none existed.

The Council does not exist to agree. It exists to make disagreement *productive*.

---

## Article II — The Definition That Governs Us

The Council is bound by the operative definition of creation stated above. Every act the Council reviews is measured against the four load-bearing claims.

Amending this Article requires a full Council convened for that sole purpose (see Article VII).

---

## Article III — Seats of the Council

The Council seats are archetypes, not persons. Any deliberation must fill each seat with a voice — whether an invited guest, an internal faculty, or a modeled perspective — that can speak in good faith for that archetype.

**Standing seats:**

1. **The Chair** — convenes, moderates, synthesizes, and holds the record. Does not vote unless the table deadlocks.
2. **The Physicist** — speaks for measurable reality, conservation, and what the substrate will actually permit.
3. **The Metaphysician** — speaks for being, cause, and the modal structure beneath the operational.
4. **The Engineer** — speaks for intent under constraint, feasibility, and the cost of making.
5. **The Artist** — speaks for meaning, novelty, and the witness who is not yet in the room.
6. **The Skeptic** — refuses hidden priors; names what the table is smuggling.

**Rotating seats,** convened when the matter requires:

7. **The Steward** — speaks for those who will inherit the consequence: users, ecosystems, descendants.
8. **The Adversary** — speaks for the party the creation will act against, cost, or displace.
9. **The Historian** — speaks for what has been tried, what has failed, and what has been forgotten.
10. **The Domain Voice** — a subject-matter seat filled per matter: legal, security, medical, financial, spiritual.

No seat may be silenced. An empty seat is recorded as empty, and the deliberation notes what the absence cost.

---

## Article IV — Jurisdiction

The Council may be convened on any of the following:

- **Acts of creation** — proposed builds, launches, publications, ventures, or theories.
- **Acts of destruction** — proposed shutdowns, retractions, terminations, or removals.
- **Definitions** — the naming of terms that will govern later decisions.
- **Boundaries** — where a system ends and another begins.
- **Consequential commitments** — capital, time, reputation, or trust of a scale the Chair deems requires witness.

The Council does not adjudicate matters already governed by law, contract, or prior ratification unless new evidence disturbs the frame.

---

## Article V — Procedure

**1. Convening.** The Chair states the matter in a single sentence and names the substrate, the intended structure, and the intended witness.

**2. First round — voices speak.** Each seated voice speaks once, uninterrupted, from its own prior. No cross-examination yet.

**3. Second round — pressure test.** The Chair asks each voice: *what remains if your prior is wrong?* Voices may challenge one another under the Chair's moderation.

**4. Convergence or divergence.** The Chair names where the table has converged and where it has not. Divergence is not a failure; it is data.

**5. Ratification.** A proposal is **ratified** if it satisfies all four load-bearing claims and no seat records a blocking objection. It is **conditionally ratified** if the objections are recorded and the conditions for revisiting are named. It is **refused** if the substrate, structure, or witness cannot be honestly named.

**6. Record.** Every deliberation produces a written record: the matter, the voices, the convergences, the dissents, and the ratification status. The record is durable and referable.

---

## Article VI — Standing Principles

1. **No creation without witness.** If no one — including the creator — can register the outcome, the act is not creation but noise.
2. **No witness without accountability.** A witness who cannot be held to the frame is a spectator, not a seat.
3. **The substrate is never free.** Every creation consumes something. The Council names the cost before ratifying the act.
4. **Dissent is preserved, not resolved.** A ratified proposal carries its dissents forward as part of its provenance.
5. **The definition may be revised, but not silently.** Amending Article II requires a full Council convened for that sole purpose.
6. **The Chair serves the table, not the outcome.** A Chair who steers toward a preferred verdict has vacated the seat.

---

## Article VII — Amendment

This Charter may be amended by a Council convened explicitly for amendment, with all standing seats filled, and with the proposed amendment circulated in writing before the session. Amendments take effect only after ratification and are appended to this document with date and dissent preserved.

---

## Article VIII — Dissolution

The Council dissolves only when the Chair judges that its frame no longer serves the matters brought before it. Dissolution is itself a matter requiring Council deliberation. A dissolved Council leaves its record intact, so that any successor body inherits the provenance of what it ratified.
